# 2. Target Customers

Research date: 2026-11-23. Labels are defined in [README](README.md#evidence-labels-used-in-every-file).

This file combines the market data in [01-market.md](01-market.md) with general café-industry knowledge. **The segments below are our working hypotheses [ESTIMATE].** They are not survey results from Miami. Test them on site (foot counts, intercept surveys, a pop-up) before you sign a lease.

## Facts that shape the customer base

| Fact | Label | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 70.6% of Miami-Dade residents are Hispanic or Latino | [CONFIRMED] Census | Bilingual (Spanish/English) service, menus, and marketing are basic expectations. Cuban, Colombian, Venezuelan, and other Latin coffee traditions shape taste. |
| Strong cafecito culture: ventanitas, coladas shared at the office, the City of Miami's "3:05" cafecito break | [REPORTED] | Locals drink a lot of coffee but are anchored to a **~$2** strong, sweet espresso. Specialty has to justify its price. |
| Median household income $71,753 (county) | [CONFIRMED] | Below many U.S. specialty-coffee hubs. Choose neighborhoods with higher local incomes or heavy office or tourist foot traffic. |
| 28.3M visitors in 2025, led by Colombia, Canada, Brazil, UK, and Mexico (international) and NY, DC, Boston, Chicago, and Atlanta (domestic) | [REPORTED] GMCVB | Many visitors come from cities with mature specialty scenes and expect third-wave quality. They peak in winter and spring. |
| Ages 18–24 lead past-day specialty use (46%) | [REPORTED] NCA | Gen Z and younger millennials drive drinks like iced lattes, matcha, and signature drinks, and they find cafés on social media. |
| Cold drinks dominate in summer | [REPORTED] NCA | Miami is hot all year. Expect iced and cold drinks to be most of the menu mix. |

## Candidate segments [ESTIMATE]

| Segment | Who | Needs | Best-fit areas |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Office and remote professionals** | Workers in Brickell and Downtown towers, plus remote workers who relocated (finance and tech) | Speed in the morning, mobile ordering, Wi-Fi or laptop space, a consistent flat white or cortado | Brickell, Downtown, Edgewater, Coral Gables business district |
| **Residents of new condos** | Young professionals and couples in new towers | A neighborhood "third place," weekend brunch-style visits, dog-friendly outdoor seating | Edgewater, Midtown, Brickell, Wynwood residential |
| **Coffee enthusiasts** | Pour-over and single-origin fans; some retail-bean buyers | Traceable beans, pour-over, retail bags and subscriptions | Wynwood, MiMo, Coconut Grove, Little Haiti / Design District |
| **Tourists and event-goers** | Hotel guests, Art Basel and festival visitors, cruise passengers | Visually appealing drinks and space, recognizable quality, card and Apple Pay | Miami Beach, Wynwood, Design District, Downtown/Bayside |
| **Latin "upgraders"** | Locals who drink cafecito daily and want a better version | Familiar flavors (cortadito, café con leche, colada) made with specialty beans at a fair price | Coral Gables, Little Havana edge, Doral, Kendall |
| **Students** | University of Miami (Coral Gables), FIU (west), Miami Dade College (Downtown and other campuses) | Price-sensitive, study space, long stays | Near campuses; lower ticket and longer dwell time |

## Behavior and preference notes

- **Discovery:** Toast's consumer research says **35%** of coffee-shop discovery comes from social media, almost matching word of mouth. [REPORTED] An informal Spoon University poll of Gen Z found convenience and price matter much more than social media. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Loyalty:** Only about **1%** say a loyalty program is their main reason for choosing a café. But **36%** would try a new shop for BOGO and **28%** for a free drink at sign-up. [REPORTED] Toast
- **Average ticket benchmarks:** Industry guides give $5–$8 as typical and $7–$10+ as a target. Miami's high prices ($4.18 for a regular hot coffee in Aug 2025) suggest a specialty café with food could reach **$9–$12**. [ESTIMATE]
- **Climate and outdoor seating:** Shade, misting, or AC matter. Outdoor seating works best from November to April. [ESTIMATE]
- **Transportation:** Car-dependent areas need parking or a drive-up option. Pedestrian areas (Brickell, Wynwood, South Beach) do not. [ESTIMATE]

## Research the customer agent should do next

1. Pull ACS 5-year data (income, age, households) for census tracts within a 0.5–1 mile radius of each candidate site.
2. Get foot-traffic data (Placer.ai or similar) and daytime-worker counts (Census LEHD OnTheMap).
3. Run 100+ intercept surveys or a weekend pop-up to test price points (for example, $6 vs. $7 for a latte).
