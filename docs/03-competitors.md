# 3. Competitors

Research date: 2026-11-23. Labels are defined in [README](README.md#evidence-labels-used-in-every-file).

**Caveat:** Location counts and opening dates come from press coverage and change often. Several openings were "planned" when reported and may have happened, slipped, or been cancelled. Check each one (Google Maps, the brand's website) before relying on it.

## Competitive landscape

Miami's coffee market has four tiers:

1. **Local specialty roasters and cafés:** the direct competitors.
2. **Specialty chains entering from outside** (since 2025): well-funded, design-driven, and growing fast.
3. **Mass chains:** Starbucks, Dunkin', and similar; everywhere, and they compete on convenience.
4. **Cuban cafecito windows (ventanitas) and bakeries:** cheap, strong, and part of daily culture. They set the local price anchor.

## 3. Mass chains

Starbucks and Dunkin' are everywhere in Miami-Dade. **This research did not count their locations.** The competitor agent should map them within 0.5 mile of each candidate site. They compete on speed, drive-thrus, and apps, not on quality.

## 4. Cuban coffee windows and bakeries

- **Ventanitas** (Versailles, La Carreta, and neighborhood bakeries) sell cafecito, cortadito, colada, and café con leche. [CONFIRMED] culturally (many sources).
- **Price anchor:** Average colada about **$2.06** across 20 popular windows in 2022 (AP / Miami Herald). Older lists show Versailles colada at about $1.44. [UNVERIFIED] for current prices; check them in person.
- **Takeaway:** These are not direct rivals for a $6 latte. But they shape what locals think coffee "should" cost. A specialty café that serves Cuban-style drinks made with better beans has a clear story.

## Positioning gaps to test [ESTIMATE]

1. **Specialty versions of Cuban and Latin drinks** (cortadito, colada, café con leche with traceable Latin American beans). No player clearly owns this at specialty quality.
2. **Neighborhoods with new residents but few cafés.** New condo areas (parts of Edgewater, Allapattah, North Miami, Doral) may have fewer specialty options than Wynwood or Brickell.
3. **Fast, high-quality service.** Per'La's stated focus on service suggests service is a known weak spot in the city.
4. **Retail beans and subscriptions** from a non-roasting café using a local roaster's beans, sold under a co-brand.
