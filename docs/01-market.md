# 1. Market Overview

Research date: 2026-11-23. Labels are defined in [README](README.md#evidence-labels-used-in-every-file).

## Summary

- U.S. specialty coffee demand is at or near a record high. Specialty drinks have passed traditional coffee in daily use.
- Miami is a large, growing, mostly Hispanic market. It has a strong existing coffee culture built on Cuban cafecito, plus heavy tourism.
- Miami's specialty segment is described as **growing but still young**. Since 2025 it has drawn new national and international chains, which signals demand and also more competition.
- Green coffee costs spiked in 2024–2025 and have come down sharply in late 2026. Tariffs on Brazilian coffee were removed in November 2025.

## Miami specialty-coffee scene signals

- **Young but growing.** In a November 2025 Barista Magazine piece, local owners said specialty coffee is "much more appreciated" than before but the scene is "still relatively young." [REPORTED]
- **Outside brands are arriving (2025–2026).** Black Sheep Coffee (UK) opened in Coconut Grove in August 2025 and plans Doral and Miami Beach. The Coffee (Brazil) opened its first U.S. café in Wynwood in December 2025. Naughty Coffee opened a flagship in January 2026. 7 Brew has said it plans to enter Miami. [REPORTED] Details are in [03-competitors.md](03-competitors.md).
- **Local brands are growing.** Ludlow Coffee Supply opened a second location (Brickell, 2026). Crema Gourmet has 25+ locations. Cortadito Coffee House has a fourth location. Sunshine Coffee plans 4–5 more. [REPORTED]
- **No reliable market-size figure** (dollar sales or number of cafés) for Miami specialty coffee was found. **Action:** the market agent should consider paid data (for example Placer.ai foot traffic, Yelp/Google listing counts, or a business-licence pull from the Miami-Dade Tax Collector) to count cafés by neighborhood.

## Coffee prices (input cost and menu price)

| Item | Value | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Arabica "C" futures, Nov 16, 2026 | 274.90 ¢/lb; down 12.9% in a month and 31.7% in a year | [REPORTED] Trading Economics |
| Outlook | Possible El Niño weather disruption into early 2027 is an upside risk | [REPORTED] |
| U.S. tariffs on Brazilian coffee | The 40% duty and 10% reciprocal tariff were removed in November 2025 (applies to entries from Nov 13, 2025) | [CONFIRMED] by multiple outlets (Fortune, Daily Coffee News, Sprudge) |
| Average price of a regular hot coffee in Miami, Aug 2025 | $4.18 (+~4% year over year); Florida +8.3% | [REPORTED] Axios / Toast data |
| Panther Coffee price increase (2025) | about 15%; the owner said specialty beans cost about 90% more | [REPORTED] Axios |
| Average Florida latte (June 2024) | $5.67 | [UNVERIFIED] older figure |
| Average colada at 20 Miami ventanitas (2022) | $2.06 | [UNVERIFIED] older figure |

**Implication:** Bean costs are easing from record highs. Customers have already accepted the 2025 menu price increases, so margins may improve in 2027 if commodity prices stay low. Specialty green coffee is priced above the "C" price, so lower futures help only partly.
