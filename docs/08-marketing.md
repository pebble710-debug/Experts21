# 8. Marketing

Research date: 2026-11-23. Labels are defined in [README](README.md#evidence-labels-used-in-every-file).

## What the data says

| Finding | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Social media drives about **35%** of coffee-shop discovery, nearly matching word of mouth | [REPORTED] | Toast consumer coffee report |
| **36%** would try a new shop for a buy-one-get-one offer; **28%** for a free drink at loyalty sign-up | [REPORTED] | Toast |
| Only about **1%** say loyalty is their main reason for choosing a café | [REPORTED] | Toast |
| Almost 2 in 5 Gen Z find new restaurants by **searching nearby** when hungry (maps and search matter) | [REPORTED] | Morning Consult |
| An informal Gen Z poll ranked convenience and price well above social trends | [UNVERIFIED] | Spoon University |
| App-based coffee orders reached a record high (2025) | [REPORTED] | NACS / NCA |
| Gen Z follows brands on social media more than any other age group (79%) | [REPORTED] | Farmer Bros. playbook (vendor) |

**Interpretation:** Social media gets people to try you. Location, speed, and quality bring them back. Loyalty programs are a retention tool, not a reason people choose you.

## Positioning ideas [ESTIMATE]

1. **"Miami-made specialty":** specialty-quality versions of cortadito, colada, and café con leche, plus standard third-wave drinks. Tell the story of Latin American origins (Colombia, Brazil, Central America), which also match Miami's top visitor markets.
2. **Bilingual by default:** Spanish/English menus, signs, and social posts.
3. **Built for the heat:** a strong iced and cold menu (cold brew, iced cortado, tropical seasonal drinks), plus AC and shade as selling points.
4. **Neighborhood third place:** comfortable seating, dog-friendly outdoor space, and community events (cuppings, local artists).

## Channels [ESTIMATE]

| Channel | Tactics | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Business Profile and Apple Maps** | Claim before opening, add photos and hours, ask for reviews every day | High: "coffee near me" searches |
| **Instagram and TikTok** | Build-out videos, drink close-ups, staff, latte art; work with local food creators | High |
| **Local press** | Miami New Times, Eater Miami, What Now Miami, Time Out Miami, Resident, Axios Miami. Send a press release about 4–6 weeks before opening. | High (these outlets cover new openings closely; much of this research came from them) |
| **Loyalty and mobile ordering** | Use the POS's built-in loyalty; free drink at sign-up; mobile pre-order for office workers | Medium |
| **Partnerships** | Nearby condo towers (resident perks), offices (catering coladas), gyms, coworking spaces, hotels | Medium |
| **Retail beans and wholesale** | Co-branded bags with the roaster; subscriptions | Low at first, grow later |
| **Delivery apps** | Uber Eats / DoorDash for nearby offices. Commissions of 15–30% hurt margins, so raise prices on the apps. | Low–medium |

## Launch plan [ESTIMATE]

- **8–12 weeks before opening:** Set up social accounts; post build-out content; collect emails and SMS sign-ups with a "free drink on opening week" offer.
- **4–6 weeks before:** Pitch local press; invite creators and neighbors to a preview tasting.
- **Soft opening (1–2 weeks):** Limited hours and menu. Invite condo residents and office managers.
- **Grand opening:** BOGO or free-drink day (supported by the Toast data above); local DJ; partner with the roaster.
- **First 90 days:** Ask for reviews every day; track repeat rate in the POS; adjust the menu.

## Marketing budget [ESTIMATE]

Launch: $5k–$20k (branding is separate). Ongoing: about 1–3% of revenue (for example, $10k–$25k/yr).
