## Market pay

| Data point | Value | Label |
|---|---|---|
| BLS OEWS "Fast Food and Counter Workers" (SOC 35-3023, the category that includes baristas), Miami metro, May 2025 | Median **$14.23/hr**, mean $15.27/hr, **tips not included** | [REPORTED] (from a third-party summary of BLS data; check at data.bls.gov, area 33100) |
| All occupations, Miami metro, May 2024 | Mean $31.88/hr | [CONFIRMED] BLS |
| Practical pay floor after Sept 30, 2026 | $15.00 base. Specialty cafés that want experienced baristas probably need **$15–$18/hr + tips**. Shift leads about $18–$22. Café manager about $50k–$70k/yr. | [ESTIMATE] |

The May 2025 BLS median ($14.23) is **below** the current $15 minimum. Market data from before the increase understates what you will actually pay.

## Sample staffing plan, one café [ESTIMATE]

Hours: 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., 7 days a week (77 open hours a week).

| Role | Headcount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| General manager / head barista (could be the owner at first) | 1 FT | Ordering, scheduling, quality, Certified Food Protection Manager |
| Shift leads | 2 FT | One per opening and closing shift; also certified managers for coverage |
| Baristas | 4–6 PT/FT | Two on bar at peak (7–10 a.m.), one to two at other times |
| Food prep / cashier | 1–2 PT | If the food program is more than reheating pastries |
| **Total** | **8–11 people; about 350–450 labor hours/week** | |

At an average of $16.50/hr × 400 hrs × 52 weeks ≈ **$343k in wages**, plus about 15% burden ≈ **$395k**. This is **too high** for a $600k–$900k café. **Most profitable small cafés have the owner work shifts and keep about 250–320 hours/week** (about $250k–$300k fully loaded). See the P&L in [05-operating-costs.md](05-operating-costs.md).

## Hiring market and risks

- **Ending of TPS (Temporary Protected Status) shrinks the labor pool.** On **June 25, 2026** the U.S. Supreme Court allowed the federal government to end TPS for Haitian and Syrian nationals. The Florida Restaurant & Lodging Association estimated about **93,000** Florida hospitality workers were affected. Miami operators (for example, Chef Creole) reported losing staff, higher labor costs, and fewer customers because of fear of immigration enforcement. [REPORTED] The evidence is mostly from individual businesses, not a survey.
- **I-9 and E-Verify compliance** is essential. Use E-Verify even below 25 employees if counsel recommends it (it is voluntary for smaller employers).
- **Bilingual staff** (Spanish/English) are effectively required. Portuguese and Haitian Creole help in some neighborhoods. [ESTIMATE]
- **Seasonality:** Schedule more staff in the Dec–Apr tourist season and fewer in summer (see the summer slowdown in [09-risks.md](09-risks.md)).

## Training and certification

- At least one **Certified Food Protection Manager** (accredited exam; DBPR Rule 61C-4.023). It is safer to certify every shift lead. [CONFIRMED]
- **DBPR-approved food handler training** for all food employees, renewed every 3 years. [CONFIRMED]
- Barista skills: consider SCA (Specialty Coffee Association) Barista Skills modules, or training from your wholesale roaster (many include it with a supply contract). [ESTIMATE]

## Where to hire

Indeed, Culinary Agents, Poached, Instagram, local roasters' networks, and Miami Dade College hospitality programs. [ESTIMATE]
