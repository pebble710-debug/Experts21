# 9. Biggest Risks

Research date: 2026-11-23. Labels are defined in [README](README.md#evidence-labels-used-in-every-file).

Ranked by our judgment of **likelihood × impact** for a first-time, single-unit operator [ESTIMATE]. The evidence for each risk is labeled.

| Rank | Risk | Likelihood | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Occupancy cost (rent) too high for sales | High | Severe |
| 2 | Labor cost and labor availability | High | High |
| 3 | Permit and build-out delays and cost overruns | High | High |
| 4 | Competition and saturation, including well-funded chains | Medium–High | High |
| 5 | Seasonality and summer slowdown | High | Medium |
| 6 | Hurricanes, flooding, and insurance | Medium | Severe |
| 7 | Coffee price and tariff swings | Medium | Medium |
| 8 | Local price anchor (the ~$2 cafecito) | Medium | Medium |
| 9 | Undercapitalization | Medium | Severe |

## 1. Rent

- **Evidence:** County average retail asking rent was $50.27/sq ft in Q3 2026. Prime Wynwood asks $100+. South Florida restaurant rents rose from about $60 to $100+/sq ft, with premier locations above $200. [REPORTED] Operators who closed blamed rent: "Miami is home to the highest bidder, and we are not the highest bidder" (Coney Burger, quoted by WLRN). Wynwood's Gramps closed citing rising rents and **falling foot traffic**. Even Panther Coffee closed its Sunset Harbour café in Feb 2026. [REPORTED]
- **How to reduce it:** Keep total occupancy cost at or below about 10–12% of realistic sales. Ask for a TI allowance and free rent during build-out. Cap CAM increases. Negotiate renewal options, a co-tenancy or exit clause, and a **permit contingency**. Limit the personal guarantee. Consider areas next to the hot spots, not the prime blocks.

## 2. Labor

- **Evidence:** Florida's minimum wage rose to $15 on Sept 30, 2026, and will be indexed to inflation from Jan 1, 2028. [CONFIRMED] Ending TPS (Supreme Court ruling June 25, 2026) affected about 93,000 Florida hospitality workers, according to FRLA, and Miami operators report losing staff and paying more. [REPORTED]
- **How to reduce it:** Have the owner work shifts. Design the bar for efficiency (batch brew, good workflow, mobile ordering). Cross-train staff. Pay above market for retention. Keep I-9 and E-Verify compliance clean.

## 3. Permits and build-out

- **Evidence:** DBPR plan review is required for spaces never licensed for food. DERM requires a grease trap and GDO permit for **coffee shops**. Change of use can trigger water and sewer connection charges and impact fees. Each city has its own Certificate of Use and zoning process. [CONFIRMED] Costs and timelines vary widely. [ESTIMATE]
- **How to reduce it:** Prefer second-generation café space. Before signing, get written confirmation of zoning, the existing grease trap's status, and any connection or impact fees. Hire a local expediter. Budget 9–15 months and keep a 10–20% build-out contingency.

## 4. Competition

- **Evidence:** Since 2025, new brands have entered or grown: Black Sheep (UK), The Coffee (Brazil), Naughty, Ludlow, Crema Gourmet (25+ locations), Cortadito, Sunshine, and possibly 7 Brew. Starbucks and Dunkin' are everywhere. [REPORTED] The GMCVB is quoted as saying that for every restaurant that closes in Miami-Dade, two open. [REPORTED]
- **How to reduce it:** A clear point of difference (see [08-marketing.md](08-marketing.md)). Choose a site in an underserved, growing neighborhood. Fast, friendly service.

## 5. Seasonality

- **Evidence:** Axios (May 2026) and CBS (2025) report a **summer slowdown** that leads to closures. Tourism peaks from December to April. [REPORTED]
- **How to reduce it:** Hold cash reserves for summer. Run a locals-focused summer program (loyalty pushes, iced specials, office catering). Schedule fewer staff. Hold events.

## 6. Hurricanes, flooding, insurance

- **Evidence:** Hurricane season runs June 1 to Nov 30. [CONFIRMED] Hurricane deductibles are often 2–15% of insured value. **Flood is excluded** from standard commercial property policies. [REPORTED] Premiums are high but stabilized in 2025–2026, and no hurricane made Florida landfall in 2025 or so far in 2026. [REPORTED] Stretches without storms do not lower long-term risk.
- **How to reduce it:** Get separate flood coverage (NFIP or private). Buy **business-interruption** coverage. Check the FEMA flood zone and elevation before choosing a site. Have a generator or power plan for refrigeration. Use impact glass or shutters. Write a staff storm plan.

## 7. Coffee prices and tariffs

- **Evidence:** Specialty beans cost Panther about 90% more in 2025, and menu prices rose about 15%. [REPORTED] Tariffs on Brazilian coffee were removed in November 2025. [CONFIRMED] Arabica futures were about $2.75/lb in Nov 2026, down about 32% year over year, but El Niño is an upside risk. [REPORTED] Trade policy can change quickly.
- **How to reduce it:** Sign a 6–12-month fixed-price contract with your roaster. Have more than one origin or supplier. Review menu prices every quarter. Drinks are only about 20–27% cost, so a commodity spike is manageable if you change prices quickly.

## 8. Local price anchor

- **Evidence:** A colada costs about $2 at ventanitas (2022 data); hot coffee in Miami averaged $4.18 (Aug 2025). [UNVERIFIED] / [REPORTED]
- **How to reduce it:** Explain the quality difference through the story. Offer a fairly priced specialty cafecito or colada as an entry item. Earn margin on signature drinks, food, and retail beans.

## 9. Undercapitalization

- **Evidence:** Our model puts startup cost at about $230k–$785k, and break-even at about 150–170 transactions/day at Miami rents (see [04](04-startup-costs.md) and [05](05-operating-costs.md)). [ESTIMATE]
- **How to reduce it:** Raise funding including **3–6 months of working capital**. Consider SBA 7(a) or 504 loans. Test demand first with a pop-up or cart.

## Smaller risks to watch

- **Regulatory changes:** possible changes to Miami-Dade FOG (grease) rules (a 2026 county report); future E-Verify expansion bills; inflation-indexed minimum wage from 2028. [REPORTED]
- **Parking and traffic** in car-dependent areas. [ESTIMATE]
- **Reputation:** Google and Yelp reviews strongly affect a new café. [ESTIMATE]
- **Immigration-enforcement climate** reducing foot traffic in some neighborhoods (reported in Little Haiti, 2026). [REPORTED]
