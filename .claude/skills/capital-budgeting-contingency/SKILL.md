# Capital budgeting & contingency (S24)

Shared skill for the Experts21 Miami specialty coffee shop team. Defined in `docs/expert-roster.md` as: Build cost budgets with low and high cases and a 10–20% contingency (total about $230k–$785k), and track actual spend against them.

## When to use

Use when estimating startup or expansion costs, sizing funding, approving change orders, or reporting budget status.

## Procedure

1. Start from the docs/04 Miami model categories (deposit, design, build-out, grease trap, equipment, furniture, POS, permits, legal, inventory, pre-opening payroll, marketing, insurance, working capital).
2. Replace estimates with quotes as you get them. Mark each line [ESTIMATE] or quote (vendor and date).
3. Add a 10–20% contingency on build-out and equipment. [ESTIMATE]
4. Working capital: 3–6 months of fixed costs ($60k–$150k in the model). [ESTIMATE]
5. Track budget vs. committed vs. spent. Flag any variance over 10%.

## Reference facts (from docs/, research date 2026-11-23)

- Model total: about $231k (low) to $784k (high); round planning range about $230k–$785k. [ESTIMATE] docs/04.
- Biggest swing factors: second-generation vs. raw space, and grease trap ($0–$35k). [ESTIMATE]

## Output

A budget table: `Category | Low | High | Quote | Committed | Spent | Variance`.

## Guardrails

- Government fees count as confirmed only where docs/06 marks them [CONFIRMED].
- Keep every evidence label ([CONFIRMED] / [REPORTED] / [ESTIMATE] / [UNVERIFIED]) and cite the docs/ file or source URL.
- If the research does not cover something, say so. Do not invent requirements, costs, or business facts.
