# Hurricane & business-continuity planning (S17)

Shared skill for the Experts21 Miami specialty coffee shop team. Defined in `docs/expert-roster.md` as: Plan for storms (June 1–Nov 30): flood zone, generator or power plan, cold-chain loss, staff communication, and reopening.

## When to use

Use when choosing a site, designing the build-out, buying insurance, writing SOPs, or communicating during storm season (June 1–Nov 30).

## Procedure

1. Check the site: FEMA flood zone, elevation, and storm history.
2. Physical protection: impact glass or shutters, raised equipment, a generator or power-backup plan for refrigeration and POS.
3. Insurance: separate flood policy (standard commercial property excludes flood), business interruption, and a hurricane deductible of 2–15% of insured value. [REPORTED]
4. Pre-storm checklist (72/48/24 hrs): stock decisions, data backup, secure outdoor furniture, staff contact tree, customer notice on social media.
5. Post-storm: safety check, discard food that got too warm, insurance documentation (photos, logs), reopening criteria, staff pay policy.

## Reference facts (from docs/, research date 2026-11-23)

- Atlantic hurricane season June 1–Nov 30. [CONFIRMED]
- No hurricane made Florida landfall in 2025 or, as of the research, in 2026. [REPORTED] Quiet years do not lower long-term risk.
- FL commercial insurance premiums are among the highest in the U.S. but stabilized in 2025–2026. [REPORTED]

## Output

A plan with phases (Prepare / Respond / Recover), owner, and trigger.

## Guardrails

- Insurance terms need a licensed broker. Life safety follows official county emergency orders.
- Keep every evidence label ([CONFIRMED] / [REPORTED] / [ESTIMATE] / [UNVERIFIED]) and cite the docs/ file or source URL.
- If the research does not cover something, say so. Do not invent requirements, costs, or business facts.
