# Price positioning vs. local anchors (S06)

Shared skill for the Experts21 Miami specialty coffee shop team. Defined in `docs/expert-roster.md` as: Set prices between the ~$2 colada and $4.18 average hot coffee and specialty drinks at $6+; design price tests.

## When to use

Use when setting or changing menu prices, positioning the brand, or interpreting price-sensitivity research.

## Procedure

1. Map the anchors: ventanita colada ~$2.06 (2022, [UNVERIFIED]); regular hot coffee average $4.18 in Miami (Aug 2025, [REPORTED]); Florida latte average $5.67 (June 2024, [UNVERIFIED]); competitor prices from the competitor-tracking skill.
2. Decide the role of each menu item: entry item (for example, a specialty cafecito or colada priced fairly), core items (latte, cold brew), and premium (signature, single-origin pour-over).
3. Check each price against cost (recipe-drink-costing) and the neighborhood (incomes vary a lot; county median household income $71,753 [CONFIRMED]).
4. Design a test: A/B price on intercept surveys or a pop-up (for example, $6 vs $7 latte), and track item mix and ticket in POS after launch.
5. Recommend a price list and a review cadence (quarterly, or when coffee costs move).

## Reference facts (from docs/, research date 2026-11-23)

- Panther Coffee raised prices about 15% in 2025 and said specialty beans cost about 90% more. [REPORTED] Axios.
- Arabica futures were about $2.75/lb on 2026-11-16, down about 32% year over year. [REPORTED]
- Promotions: 36% would try a shop for BOGO; 28% for a free drink at sign-up. [REPORTED] Toast.

## Output

A price ladder table: `Item | Role | Price | Cost % | Nearest competitor price | Rationale`.

## Guardrails

- Current competitor prices must be checked in person or online; do not assume them.
- Keep every evidence label ([CONFIRMED] / [REPORTED] / [ESTIMATE] / [UNVERIFIED]) and cite the docs/ file or source URL.
- If the research does not cover something, say so. Do not invent requirements, costs, or business facts.
